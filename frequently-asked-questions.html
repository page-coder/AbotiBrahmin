Who are the Aboti Brahmins?

The Aboti Brahmins constitute a historically localized Brahmin community of western India, primarily concentrated in the Saurashtra peninsula of Gujarat (notably Dwarka, Porbandar, Ranavav, and Junagadh regions) with medieval epigraphic and textual references linking them to Dvaravati (Dwarka) and southern Rajasthan.

What does this encyclopedia say about the community’s history and origins?

Tracing the historical trajectory of the Aboti Brahmins requires examining medieval temple-service records in Rajasthan (c. 1228 CE / VS 1306) and Dwarka (14th–16th centuries CE), colonial ethnographic classifications linking them with Shrimali and Sakaldwipiya streams, and their eventual consolidation as a temple-serving and agricultural community in coastal Saurashtra.

What is the connection with Dwarka?

Situated at the western extremity of Gujarat's Saurashtra peninsula where the Gomti River meets the Arabian Sea, Dwarka (ancient Dvaraka or Dvaravati) is one of Hinduism's four Char Dham pilgrimage seats and seven sacred Sapta Puri cities. This article examines its geography, the 15th–16th century Maru-Gurjara Dwarkadhish Temple, terrestrial and underwater archaeological findings (1963–2025), and how the town's sacred ecology shaped priestly communities including the Aboti and Gugali Brahmins.

Which temples and sacred places are discussed?

The sacred landscape connected with the Aboti Brahmin community spans pan-Indian pilgrimage monuments in Okhamandal—most notably the Dwarkadhish Temple (Jagat Mandir), Gomti Ghat, Rukmini Devi Temple, Bet Dwarka, and Nageshwar Jyotirlinga—as well as community dharamshalas in Dwarka and village shrines in Ranavav taluka. What is the difference between a gotra and a surname (atak)?

Understanding kinship in western Indian Brahmin communities requires distinguishing between exogamous ritual clans (gotra and pravara) and regional family surnames (atak), which often derive from ancestral villages in Saurashtra or priestly titles. This reference article explains the classical gotra system, lists documented Aboti Brahmin surnames and ancestral villages from published community registries, and establishes ethical protocols for genealogical research.

Does a surname prove that someone is an Aboti Brahmin?

No. A surname alone never establishes caste membership or gotra lineage; this needs corroborating family vanshavali, kuldevi and village-branch records. Several surnames also occur in neighbouring communities.

Is personal information about living people published?

No. The archive does not publish phone numbers, home addresses, private e-mail addresses or unconsented family trees of living people, and uses only collective information such as surname categories and ancestral villages.
