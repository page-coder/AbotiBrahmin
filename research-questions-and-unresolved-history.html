Research Questions and Unresolved History

A structured ledger of six open historical, epigraphic, and sociological questions requiring primary archival verification.

Encyclopedic overview: In any serious historical encyclopedia, intellectual honesty is measured by how transparently the work identifies what is not yet proven. Rather than smoothing over gaps with invented chronologies, this section catalogs six unresolved questions in Aboti Brahmin history—detailing for each question what is currently known, what traditional accounts state, what evidence exists, and what specific archival leads require investigation.
Why Document Uncertainty? Presenting unresolved questions openly prevents the spread of internet myths and equips university students, local historians, and community researchers with concrete archival targets.

1. Inquiry I & II: The 1228 CE Rajasthan Record and the 1557 CE Dwarka Temple Settlement

Needs Verification

Research Question 1 — The 1228 CE (Vikram Samvat 1306) Rajasthan Record:

  • The Historical Question: Which specific stone inscription, copper plate, or manuscript colophon from 1228 CE (VS 1306) in Rajasthan records the Aboti Brahmins as temple servants migrated from Dvaravati?
  • What Is Currently Known: Om Prakash's Cultural History of India (2005, p. 313) and Wikipedia's article on Aboti Brahmins cite 1228 CE (1306 VS) in Rajasthan as the earliest documented benchmark for the community, referencing Anita Sudan's A Study of the Cahamana Inscriptions of Rajasthan (1989) in the accompanying bibliography [1].
  • What Additional Evidence Is Needed: Secondary encyclopedias state the date and summary without quoting the original Sanskrit/Prakrit epigraphic lines, the exact find-spot (e.g., Bhinmal, Jalor, Nadol, or Mount Abu), or the ruling Cahamana monarch's regnal year. Locating the exact corpus entry in Sudan (1989) or Epigraphia Indica is a high-priority task for archival verification.

Research Question 2 — The 1345 CE Dispute and 1557 CE (VS 1613) Dwarka Copper Plate:

  • The Historical Question: Where is the physical repository or published transcription of the Vikram Samvat 1613 (1557 CE) copper-plate settlement attributed to Goswami Vitthalanathji mediating temple income shares between Aboti and Gugali Brahmins at Dwarka?
  • What Is Currently Known: Secondary historical syntheses on Dwarka and the Aboti community refer to a 1345 CE dispute over temple offerings and a formal 1557 CE (VS 1613) resolution during the sixteenth-century reorganization of the Dwarkadhish Temple [2].
  • What Additional Evidence Is Needed: A critical edition showing the original text, signatory names, and exact proportional shares (annas/paisa per rupee of offering) from the Dwarka temple trust archives, Pushtimarg sampradaya varta literature, or Baroda/Okhamandal state judicial records.

2. Inquiry III & IV: Sub-Caste Affinities and Rural Settlement Chronology in Porbandar

Needs Verification

Research Question 3 — Reconciling Shrimali, Sakaldwipiya, and Bardai Classifications:

  • The Historical Question: Why did R. E. Enthoven (1920) classify Aboti as a subdivision of Shrimali Brahmans, whereas Om Prakash (2005) classified them as Sakaldwipiya Brahmans, and some local Saurashtra accounts suggest proximity to Bardai or Gugali Brahmans?
  • What Is Currently Known: Each classification reflects a genuine historical contact zone—Shrimal/Marwar in southern Rajasthan (Enthoven), temple-priestly Sakaldwipiya/Bhojaka service in western India (Prakash), and geographic co-residence in the Barda Hills / Porbandar and Dwarka regions [1][2][3].
  • What Additional Evidence Is Needed: Comparative study of traditional marriage boundaries (gnati bandhan), Sandhya/Veda-shakha liturgy (e.g., Shukla Yajurveda Madhyandina vs. Samaveda Kauthuma), and nineteenth-century Kathiawar Political Agency caste returns.

Research Question 4 — Chronology of Settlement in Ranavav, Sheel, and Keshod:

  • The Historical Question: In which century did Aboti Brahmin families first establish permanent agricultural settlements in Ranavav taluka villages (Bhod, Mokar, Rana Vadvala, Tukda Gosa, Vanana) and in Junagadh's Sheel and Keshod mahals?
  • What Is Currently Known: By the 1901 Census of Junagadh State, 970 Aboti Brahmins were already an established cultivating population in Sheel and Keshod mahals, and Ranavav taluka villages represent multi-generational ancestral homelands (vatan) [2][4].
  • What Additional Evidence Is Needed: Inspection of pre-1900 Porbandar State (Jethwa rulers) and Junagadh State revenue registers (hak-patraks and pasaita/barkhali temple-and-service land grant ledgers) in the Gujarat State Archives (Rajkot and Junagadh branches).

3. Inquiry V & VI: Comprehensive Gotra-Kuldevi Mapping and Contemporary Demographics

Needs Verification

Research Question 5 — Systematic Gotra, Pravara, and Kuldevi Table by Surname:

  • The Historical Question: What is the complete, verified correspondence between each Aboti Brahmin surname (Shiyani, Mokariya, Odedara, Joshi, Kuchhadiya, Tukadiya, Bhuva, Borichiya, Baleja, Jamariya, Goriya, Kothari, Mehta, Poriya) and its traditional Gotra, Pravara, and Kuldevi shrine?
  • What Is Currently Known: Community sources confirm patrilineal exogamy, devotion to Parshuram and Gopal, and specific Kuldevi shrines such as Aval Mataji at Rana Vadvala and Gatral Mataji, while secondary encyclopedias note that a complete academic gotra table has not yet been published [2][5].
  • What Additional Evidence Is Needed: Collaborative verification with village elders, family priests, and Barot vanshavali registers across Ranavav and Dwarka.

Research Question 6 — Contemporary Population and Diaspora Distribution:

  • The Historical Question: What is the current total population and household count of the Aboti Brahmin community across Gujarat and the pan-Indian urban diaspora?
  • What Is Currently Known: The last published state census figure for a single princely state was 970 individuals in Junagadh State in 1901 (excluding Porbandar State and Okhamandal/Baroda State) [2]. Modern community directories and the Aboti Brahmin Vikas Trust in Porbandar connect ratusan/thousands of households across Saurashtra, Surat, Vadodara, Rajkot, Ahmedabad, Mumbai, Pune, Delhi, Chennai, and Kolkata [2][5].
  • What Additional Evidence Is Needed: Publication of anonymized aggregate household statistics compiled by the Aboti Brahmin Vikas Trust (ABVT) and regional samaj mandals.
