About the Archive & Editorial Policy

Mission statement, four-tier epistemic classification system, visual attribution policy, bilingual translation standards, and corrections protocol.

Encyclopedic overview: This charter defines the editorial, historiographical, and ethical standards governing the AbotiBrahmin Digital Encyclopedia. The archive is an independent public-interest knowledge resource—not an official community authority, temple trust, or academic institution—and commits strictly to distinguishing documented evidence from oral tradition.
Institutional Transparency: AbotiBrahmin is an independent digital encyclopedia and heritage archive. It does not claim to be a government body, statutory temple board, or accredited university department.

1. Mission, Scope, and Institutional Independence

Documented

The AbotiBrahmin Digital Encyclopedia is an independent, non-promotional reference archive dedicated to documenting the history, sacred geography, religious traditions, kinship structures, and socio-economic evolution of the Aboti Brahmin community in Dwarka, Saurashtra, Gujarat, and the wider Indian diaspora. It does not claim to speak as an official judicial authority of the community, nor as a government agency, temple board, or accredited university department.

In accordance with encyclopedic best practices, no fictional editorial boards, fabricated academic titles, invented publication metrics, or artificial visitor counts appear anywhere on this platform. Every article stands solely on the quality of its prose, the transparency of its epistemic badges, and the verifiability of its cited sources.

2. Four-Tier Epistemic Classification & Visual Attribution Standards

Documented

Every section across all thirteen encyclopedia articles is tagged with one of four editorial classification badges:

  • [Documented] (દસ્તાવેજીકૃત): Claims directly supported by traceable primary or secondary publications, such as the 1901 Census of Junagadh State, R. E. Enthoven (1920), Om Prakash (2005), H. D. Sankalia / Z. D. Ansari & M. S. Mate (1966), S. R. Rao (1988, 1999), or the CMPA Gosabara Wetland Technical Report (2018).
  • [Traditional Account] (પરંપરાગત વૃત્તાંત): Narratives rooted in Sanskrit Puranic literature, devotional memory, or oral community heritage (such as veneration of Bhagwan Parshuram, Puranic accounts of Krishna's Dvaravati, or oral lineage traditions).
  • [Interpretation] (વિદ્વાનોનું અર્થઘટન): Analytical or methodological syntheses explaining how historians, philologists, and sociologists interpret available evidence.
  • [Needs Verification] (ચકાસણી જરૂરી): Specific historical claims or archival leads (such as untranscribed medieval copper plates or complete surname-to-gotra tables) that await primary documentary publication.

Visual Policy: All seven supplied visuals—two photographs of the Dwarkadhish Temple and Gomti River in Dwarka, the Raja Ravi Varma Press chromolithograph of Bhagwan Parshuram (Reg. No. 679), the Ravi Varma Press chromolithograph of the Saptarishi performing yajna (Reg. No. 628), two illustrative paintings of Vedic rishis and gurukula assemblies, and the documentary portrait of a Hindu ascetic—are used strictly as representative contextual visuals with transparent captions. No illustration or photograph is ever presented as proof of a disputed genealogical claim.

3. Submitting Corrections, Archival Sources, and Translations

Documented

Because regional history advances when new inscriptions, state gazetteers, or family documents come to light, readers, historians, and community elders are invited to submit source-backed corrections or archival additions through the repository's issue tracker or documented contribution workflow. To be upgraded to [Documented] status, a submission must include a verifiable bibliographic citation, archival call number, or clear photograph/transcription of a primary inscription or state record.

Bilingual Equivalency Commitment: Both the English (lang='en') and Gujarati (lang='gu') editions of this encyclopedia contain complete, human-reviewed long-form prose across all thirteen articles and navigation interfaces. Technical Sanskrit and Gujarati terms (such as gotra, pravara, atak, shikhara, sabhamandapa, pasaita, and angadia) are preserved with contextual explanations rather than distorted through mechanical translation.
